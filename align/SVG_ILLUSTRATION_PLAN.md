## Direction

Use a small family of original geometric illustrations to explain each service and balance unused space beside the copy. Preserve generous whitespace between sections. Add one primary illustration per interior page; avoid repeating the same illustration at the hero, appointment section and footer.

This placement plan is based on current templates, CSS and the screenshots already provided. Exact open-space measurements need a rendered review when browser access is available; the earlier browser approval restriction remains unresolved.

## Shared visual language

- Navy `#17324D`: principal outlines and one party.
- Sage `#6F8F72`: complementary shapes and the second party.
- Warm paper `#F7F7F2`: negative space; pale surface `#EDF0E9` where needed.
- Neutral red `#B84032`: only a single mediator/neutral between two parties where that relationship is represented. Do not use it as a general decoration or status colour.
- Use the existing darker sage `#59775C` for any supporting text; keep labels as HTML.
- Combine restrained solid forms with consistent 1.5–2px strokes at displayed size. Rounded line caps, flat fills, no gradients, shadows or texture.
- Keep the supplied logo unchanged. Illustrations complement it and are not new logos.
- Begin with static artwork. No scroll-triggered animation, draggable graphics or new interaction is needed for this request.

## Shared sections and whitespace

- Header and footer: retain the actual ALIGN logo. Do not fill their open areas with extra drawings; contact links, navigation and location need clear separation.
- Service appointment panels: keep the existing text/metadata layout. Do not add a third column of artwork.
- Registry notes and related-service lists: no repeated large illustrations. Their purpose is the next action.
- Homepage cost curve: retain the existing diagram and readable mobile stages. Do not add decorative artwork to the chart.
- Homepage navy introduction and closing statement: let typography carry them; avoid filling intentional margins.
- Do not restore removed slogans, claims, labels or sections as SVG text.

## Responsive placement rules

1. Keep every interior-page H1 full width so a graphic cannot force long headings into narrow columns.
2. Below the H1, introduce an optional two-column row for the standfirst and illustration at widths of roughly 1000px or greater. Start with a 2:1 split, 40–56px gap and 240–320px maximum artwork width.
3. Below that breakpoint, stack the illustration after the introduction. Use a 160–230px width and roughly 100–160px maximum visual height, adapting each drawing's proportions.
4. Use normal grid/flex document flow, intrinsic SVG aspect ratios and reserved dimensions. No absolute placement over text and no forced full-screen section heights.
5. On short screens, reduce decorative artwork before reducing text or control sizes. Preserve natural text enlargement.
6. Reuse service symbols as simplified derivatives; do not shrink a detailed illustration until it becomes unreadable.

## Accessibility and technical approach

- Hand-code local SVGs with consistent viewBoxes and colour tokens. Use a small route-to-illustration mapping in the static builder.
- Artwork repeating the adjacent copy is decorative: `aria-hidden="true"`, `focusable="false"`, no tab stops. If a future diagram adds information, provide an accessible name and a complete HTML equivalent.
- Do not encode necessary distinctions using navy versus sage alone. Shape and position should carry the metaphor too.
- Keep text out of paths and SVG labels to avoid another tiny-chart-text problem.
- Give inline SVG definitions unique IDs if needed. Prefer simple paths without filters, embedded images, external requests or scripts.
- Aim for under 5 KB per primary illustration; treat this as a budget to verify, not a measured result.
- Preserve navigation, scrolling highlights, footer links and all existing service copy.
